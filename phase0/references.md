Prototype shell 72.8 kgCO2eq/m2 plus 15.5 for the levelled floor, total 88.3. 48% below a 250 mm flat slab. A later scenario in the same paper is 53 kgCO2eq/m2 and 60% below that slab. |
| Feickert & Mueller, Architecture, Structures and Construction 4 (2024) 15-36, online 4 Nov 2023, 10.1007/s44150-023-00101-z | PDF read 20 Nov 2026. Shell footings against spread footings. About half the embodied carbon at small loads on weak soil, and up to about two-thirds at about 5 MN. With shape-optimised floors the frame GWP falls 72% on the soil studied. The lateral system is not in the model. |
| Chiang & Borgart 2021, Eng. Struct. 251 (online 2021), 10.1016/j.engstruct.2021.113514. Actual title: "A form-finding method for membrane shells with radial basis functions" | Papers/10.1016_j.engstruct.2021.113514.pdf. Claim checked in the abstract: "peripheral walls are preferable to allow the membrane shells to carry horizontal loads in various directions" |
| Jewett & Carstensen 2026, arXiv:2604.22070 | PDF read 20 Nov 2026. Tested reinforced-concrete beams. Same material, loads 36-42% above the conventional beam. About 33% less material at the same performance and depth. No seismic load case and no floor. The PDF affiliation is MIT. |
| Kotronis & Grange 2010, European Journal of Environmental and Civil Engineering 14(6-7) 823-838, 10.1080/19648189.2010.9693264 | Title from HAL: Simplified modelling strategies for reinforced concrete structures.
